# Portfolio audit: websitesbymanny.netlify.app

Reviewed 20 September 2026 against the current build, served from `dist/` at 1440x900 and 375x812, in both themes. Every measurement below was taken from the running site or the source, not estimated.

---

## 0. The diagnosis in one page

**One-sentence diagnosis.** This is a well-built, unusually honest site that is organised as an autobiography when it needed to be an argument, so a busy visitor meets your name, then your life story, then seventeen equally-weighted work samples, and never once meets a number that proves you are good at this.

**One-sentence target state.** A visitor should learn in five seconds that you build and market websites for software companies, see proof that you took one client from 4,000 to 30,000 organic visitors a month before they scroll, and reach three pieces of work chosen to make that claim credible.

**The five facts that drive everything else in this document:**

| Measurement | Value |
|---|---|
| Homepage height, desktop 1440x900 | 13,027px, about 14.5 screens |
| Homepage height, mobile 375x812 | 20,477px, about 25 screens |
| Distance from top to the first piece of work | 3,003px |
| Work samples on the homepage | 17, all the same visual weight |
| Numeric results shown on the homepage | 0 |

The single best asset you own is the TimeClick SEO result, 4,000 to 30,000+ monthly organic visitors, sourced to Google Analytics and dated. It appears in `src/data/project-walkthroughs.ts:32,47` and renders on the case study page. It appears nowhere on the homepage. The homepage card for TimeClick, `src/data/projects.ts:37`, ends on "The goal is to get the right visitors to start a free trial", which is an intention, not an outcome.

**What is genuinely excellent, and must not be lost.** The craft is real and better than most portfolios at this level. Colour contrast passes everywhere I measured, with a worst case of 5.93:1 on the hero's recent-work line and typical body text at 7.5:1 to 8.6:1. Reduced-motion is honoured in four separate places, including a JavaScript guard that stops videos downloading at all. All 233 images carry alt text. There are zero broken internal links, zero console errors, one `h1` per page, and a working skip link. The honesty is a real differentiator: "built without being asked" on three concept redesigns, and an invented finance firm labelled as invented in four places. Keep all of it.

## 1. First impressions

Tested at 1440x900. The hero is exactly 820px tall under an 80px header, so the fold lands precisely at the bottom of the hero. Above it you see: the logo, the `h1`, one paragraph, two buttons, one grey line of client names. No work, no image, no proof.

**At 3 seconds.** A man named Manny Pachano has a portfolio. The dominant object on the screen is his own name, set 56px, weight 900, uppercase, letter-spaced 2px, centred. The second most dominant object is a yellow button.

**At 10 seconds.** He is a front-end developer and a marketing specialist. He builds websites for software companies and small businesses and does SEO, email and CRM. He has worked with US clients since 2019.

**At 30 seconds.** Still the same, plus three client names in low-contrast grey at the very bottom of the hero: TimeClick, Universal Environmental Services, nuVector, "and several small businesses in the US". Nothing yet suggests whether he is any good.

**The direct answers you asked for:**

- *What do you do?* Two things, and the site never chooses. "Front-end developer and marketing specialist" is two job titles joined by "and". Compounding it, `src/config.ts:6` makes the page title "Front-End Developer & Marketing Specialist", the About section lists a third identity (founder of Clox) and a fourth (freelance since 2019).
- *Who for?* "software companies and small businesses" is two very different buyers with different budgets, cycles and vocabularies.
- *Strongest skill?* Unanswerable from the first screen. After a full read, it is SEO and marketing attached to front-end work, which is genuinely unusual and is the most valuable thing about you. The site never says so.
- *Value proposition?* Absent. The lede describes activities, not outcomes.
- *Desired action?* "See my work" (yellow, primary) or "Get in touch" (dark, secondary). Reasonable and clear.
- *Confusing?* The headline. "Hey, I'm Manny Pachano" is a greeting, not a proposition, and it is the most casual string on a site whose nav, title and logo are all formal.
- *Compelling?* The two-button pairing and the client names. The names are the only credibility in the viewport.
- *Generic?* The headline, the lede, and the "several small businesses" hedge.
- *What would make you leave?* Needing to scroll 3,003px to see one piece of work.
- *Unanswered.* Are you available, where are you, do you want a job or a contract, what does it cost, has anyone ever said you were good.

**Do the hero elements work together?** Partly. The type hierarchy is clean and the buttons are unambiguous. Two problems:

1. **The hero illustration is invisible.** `src/components/Hero.astro:122-125` paints `/images/manny-img.webp`, 108KB, on a div with `opacity: 0`. The only rule that reveals it is `.hero-section:has(h1:hover) .manny-img { opacity: 1 }`. So a 108KB illustration downloads on every visit and appears only while the cursor sits on the headline. On touch it can never appear at all. A scripted "peek" at `Hero.astro:157-164` flashes it between 1000ms and 2400ms to teach the hover.
2. **The headline blinks.** That same timer adds and removes a class on the `h1` at 1500, 1720, 1900 and 2120ms. Your headline flickers twice during the exact window in which a visitor decides whether to stay.

**Fix.** Delete the hover reveal and the timer. Either show the image or remove it. Then rewrite the headline to make a claim, and put one number under it. Replacement copy is in section 19.

## 2. Positioning and personal brand

**What the site communicates.** A capable generalist who does web work. Honest, thorough, quietly technical, not selling hard.

**Which category does it place you in?** It reads as *a personal portfolio*, not a freelancer's business site and not a specialist's site. Signals that cause the ambiguity:

| Signal | Reads as |
|---|---|
| "Hey, I'm Manny Pachano" as the `h1` | Personal portfolio |
| An employment history block with four roles, `About.astro:6-33` | Job seeker, CV |
| A services checklist in the contact form, `Contact.astro:6-9` | Freelancer taking client work |
| "My own product. Clox" and "My own project" on two cards | Indie founder |
| A cursor trail of ten dots, `global.css:319-337` | Creative portfolio, 2015 |
| Six unsolicited concept redesigns | Someone building a case to be hired |

Those six audiences want different things and the site serves them in one undifferentiated scroll.

**What makes you different.** You do front-end development *and* the marketing that makes it pay, and you have a measured result to prove the combination works. Almost no front-end portfolio can show an organic traffic curve. That is your position and it is currently buried on a sub-page.

**What someone remembers after leaving.** Honestly, the yellow, and that you seem thorough. Not a number, not a specialism, not a phrase.

**Does the visual identity match?** Mostly. Black, cream and a single high-chroma yellow is a confident, legible system, applied consistently. The cursor trail and the hidden hover illustration are the two elements pulling toward "personal creative site" and away from "person you hire to grow a software company's traffic". That is a taste judgement, not a defect, but it works against the positioning I would recommend.

## 3. Information architecture

**Current order.** Hero, About, Projects, Landing pages, Contact, Footer.

**The central problem.** About occupies 2,103px between the hero and the first work sample. It contains three subsections: a biography of four paragraphs, a skills and tools grid of four groups, and an employment history of four roles. A prospective client does not need your employment history before seeing your work, and a recruiter does not need your skills grid before seeing whether you can build.

**Section heights, desktop:**

| Section | Height | Share of page |
|---|---|---|
| Hero | 820px | 6% |
| About | 2,103px | 16% |
| Projects (11 cards) | 4,750px | 36% |
| Landing pages (6 cards) | 3,574px | 27% |
| Contact | 1,262px | 10% |
| Footer | 439px | 3% |

**Projects and Landing pages are the same thing to a visitor.** Both are "work Manny made". Splitting them into two sections separated by a heading creates 8,324px of undifferentiated browsing, and forces a visitor to understand a taxonomy that only matters to you. The distinction that actually matters is *paid client work with results* versus *self-initiated concept work*, and the current labels do not say that.

**Nav.** Five items is fine. Two mismatches: nav says "Projects", the section heading says "Recent projects"; nav says "Contact", the heading says "Get in touch". Align them. The full link row now starts at 901px, with the burger below it, which is correct for five items.

**Recommended structure.** Full proposal in section 18. The short version: hero with proof, three selected projects, the credibility block, everything else behind "see all work", then a short about, then contact.

## 4. User journeys

**A potential client, arriving from a link you sent.** Wants to know you can do the job and that hiring you is safe. Needs: evidence of similar work, a result, a price posture, a way to reach you. Hesitates at the About wall. Drops off around the seventh identical project card. Should end at the form; currently there is no budget field, no timeline field, and no alternative to the form except a LinkedIn icon in the footer.

**A recruiter or hiring manager.** Wants your stack, your level, your code, and whether you are available. Needs: a repo link, a CV, a location, a statement of what you want next. The site has one GitHub URL and it is a plain-text fact row on the Keyset case study, `project-walkthroughs.ts:999`, not a link in the footer. There is no CV, no location, and no availability statement anywhere. This journey currently fails.

**Someone discovering you through Google.** This journey cannot happen. `src/layouts/Layout.astro:34` emits `<meta name="robots" content="noindex, nofollow, noarchive">` on all 19 built pages, and `netlify.toml:21` adds `X-Robots-Tag` with the same directives. The comment above it says the portfolio is shared by link only. That is a deliberate decision and I am not second-guessing it, but it does mean you asked me to audit a discovery channel you have switched off. Decide which you want, because half the value of the case study depth you have built is wasted while the site is unlisted.

**Someone who already knows you.** Best-served journey. They scan work, recognise the quality, and leave. Fine as is.

**Someone checking whether you are credible.** Wants third-party confirmation. There are zero testimonials on the site. The only quotes anywhere are quotes inside the client sites you describe. Nobody vouches for you in your own voice or theirs. This is the largest single credibility gap.

## 5. Cognitive load and friction

**Seventeen work items with no ranking.** Eleven projects plus six landing pages, every card the same size, so the visitor must evaluate all of them. Three of the eleven are explicitly not client work: Keyset ("there is no client"), MMA Class Tracker ("my friends and I"), and Clox (your own product). Landscaper Templates is a speculative template set. So roughly a third of the "Recent projects" wall is not client delivery, and nothing tells the visitor which is which before they read.

**Six tags per card.** The TimeClick card carries WordPress, JavaScript, Zoho CRM, Stripe & WooCommerce, SEO, Email marketing. Multiplied by 11 cards, that is roughly 60 grey pills competing with the sentences that actually matter.

**Two different case study templates, three to four times apart in depth.** Measured from the build:

| Template | Pages | Words | Images |
|---|---|---|---|
| Walkthrough (newer) | 12 | 1,039 to 1,623 | 13 to 25 |
| CaseStudyLayout (older) | 5 | 336 to 467 | 1 to 2 |

The five thin pages are nuVector, LoopManager, Explainer Visuals, Panda's Playhouse and MMA Class Tracker. So nuVector, a real company you worked for across three years, gets 467 words and one image, while Keyset, a personal exercise with no client, gets 1,202 words and 13 images. The portfolio's depth is inverted against the work's professional weight. A visitor who clicks two cards in a row will feel the drop.

**Repetition.** The footer paragraph, `Footer.astro:22`, restates the hero lede almost verbatim. "I designed, built and market it myself" appears in both `About.astro:98` and `projects.ts:72`. Three landing summaries open with the identical construction "A redesign of the homepage for a… built without being asked… What changed is…". Nine of eleven project summaries open "I" plus a verb, six of them "I built".

**Ambiguity worth fixing.** "The data is generated and there is no client", `projects.ts:90`. On a page selling client work, "there is no client" can be misread as a statement about your business.

**Jargon aimed at the wrong reader.** Your stated audience includes small businesses. "optimistic saves that roll back when the server refuses" and "converted the videos to HLS playlists" are engineer-to-engineer sentences sitting on cards a business owner is meant to scan.

## 6. Visual design

**Type.** One family throughout, Source Sans 3, at five weights. Scale in use: 14.4, 16, 18, 20, 24, 40, 56px. That is a clean, restrained scale and the hierarchy reads. Line-heights are generous, 1.8 on body copy.

Two issues:
1. **Centred multi-line body copy.** The hero lede is centred and runs to six lines at 375px. Centred text with a ragged left edge is measurably slower to read. Left-align it below about 600px.
2. **2px letter-spacing on uppercase display headings** at 40 and 56px. Positive tracking on large uppercase text is a deliberate look and it is applied consistently; it also makes the headline physically wider, which is part of why the mobile `h1` takes three lines.

**Colour and contrast.** This is the strongest part of the design. Measured pairs, dark theme:

| Element | Foreground on background | Ratio |
|---|---|---|
| Hero `h1` | `#f3f1ee` on `#1a1917` | 15.58 |
| Hero lede | `#ddd9d3` on `#1a1917` | 12.50 |
| Card summary | `#bab5ae` on `#262422` | 7.59 |
| Nav link | `#bab5ae` on `#1a1917` | 8.62 |
| Primary button | `#111111` on `#ffe83d` | 15.16 |
| Hero recent-work line | `#9c958c` on `#1a1917` | 5.93 |

Light theme is equally strong, worst measured case 7.46:1 on card summaries. Nothing fails. The weakest, 5.93:1, is on the one line in the hero carrying your client names, which is the opposite of the emphasis it deserves.

**Visual hierarchy.** Inside a card, hierarchy is correct: meta line, title, summary, tags, primary button, secondary link. Across the page it collapses. Every one of the 17 cards has identical weight, so there is no "look at this one". The fix is not styling, it is selection.

**Consistency.** At a glance the system looks tight. Measured, it has drifted in four places a visitor can actually see.

1. **Section headings are two different sizes for the same tag.** On the homepage an `h2` renders at 40px, weight 700, uppercase. On all seventeen case study and walkthrough pages an `h2` renders at 24px, weight 800, not uppercase, because `h3, .section-title` in `global.css:250-259` has class specificity and beats the bare `h2` rule. Every section heading on a sub-page therefore sits a full visual tier below every section heading on the homepage.
2. **Three different page gutters.** At 1440px, About, Projects and Landing pages sit 40px from the viewport edge, while the contact form and footer sit at 16px, and case study pages use 48px. The shared rule in `global.css:741-748` was written to fix this, but Astro's component scoping gives `Contact.astro` and `Footer.astro` higher specificity, so it never applies to them. The contact form and footer are visibly 24px wider than the sections directly above them.
3. **The two card types behave differently.** `ProjectCard.astro` lifts on hover and on `:focus-within`, with a 0.3s transition and `overflow: hidden`. `LandingPageCard.astro` has no hover, no focus-within, no transition and `overflow: visible`. They sit in adjacent sections and read as the same component, so one wall of cards responds to the cursor and the next does not. The landing cards also reflow at 1100px while project cards reflow at 901px, so between those widths the two sections use different layouts.
4. **Seven button geometries.** The homepage hero CTA is 20px text in a 3.5rem pad; the case study hero CTA in the same screen position is 18px in a 2.5rem pad. The contact submit is the widest button on the site with the smallest label.

Underneath that, the token system is half-built. There are 29 colour tokens and a genuinely good literal-versus-semantic split, but no spacing tokens at all behind 27 distinct spacing steps, one radius token behind 11 untokenised radii, and 20 distinct font sizes where about eight would do. Four of those sizes fall inside a 0.8px band, and `1.375rem` and `1.4rem` differ by 0.4px.

**Polish.** It does not feel amateur, template-like or AI-generated. It feels hand-built, and the honesty notes make it feel human. Two elements read as decoration rather than design: the ten-dot cursor trail, and the hidden hover illustration. Both are taste calls. My recommendation is to cut both, because they spend attention on flourish in a site whose problem is that it does not make a case.

## 7. Copy

Your copy is cleaner than almost any portfolio I could compare it to. There are no buzzwords, no clichés, no exclamation marks, no em dashes, no "passionate about". Do not let the criticisms below suggest otherwise. The problems are structural, not stylistic.

**Every project summary states what it is and what you did. None states a result.** Eleven of eleven. Scored across four axes, the summaries answer "what is it" 11 times, "who for" 5 times in the sentence itself, "what did you do" 11 times, and "what happened" 0 times.

**Claims running without evidence, quoted:**

- "I build websites for software companies and small businesses, and I handle the SEO, email and CRM work behind them." No proof in the viewport.
- "several small businesses in the US". Unnamed and uncounted, sitting next to three named clients.
- "I rebuilt their homepage and I run their ongoing SEO program." The case study itself says the programme is in its second month with no results yet, which is honest, but the homepage does not say it.
- "so the marketing team could publish posts without a developer". No count of posts, no link.
- "They are intentionally generic so a business owner can compare the layouts and the speed first." Speed is the pitch and no speed number is given, although you have page-weight figures for every landing page.

**Numbers you already own and do not show on the homepage:** 4,000 to 30,000+ monthly organic visitors; 2,400 keywords ranking with 472 in the top three; 753 Google AI Overviews citing the client; 8,000+ businesses using TimeClick; 35,000+ customers and 28 states for UES; 132 tests and Lighthouse 100 for Keyset; page weights from 52KB to 561KB across the landing pages.

**Voice.** First person everywhere except three places. All six landing-page card summaries are agentless and contain no "I", while the project cards beside them are all first person. The Experience block is a third-person CV in title case with ampersands inside an otherwise first-person section. About drops contractions that the hero, contact and footer all use, so it reads stiffer than everything around it.

**One CTA, four names.** "See my work", "Get in touch" (button), "Get in touch" (the heading it scrolls to), "Send me a message", "Send message". Clicking a button labelled "Get in touch" and arriving at a heading reading "Get in touch" is a small, avoidable moment of "did that do anything".

## 8. Conversion

**Primary goal.** A contact form submission. **Secondary.** A case study read.

**Why someone would contact you.** The work looks competently made, the concept redesigns show initiative, the honesty is disarming, and the response promise is concrete, one business day.

**Why someone would not.** They cannot tell what you cost, whether you are available, whether you want a job or a project, or whether anyone has been happy with you before. They have to write a free-text message with no guidance on what to include, and the only alternative channel is a LinkedIn icon labelled "Social".

**Form friction, measured.** Seven inputs, three required, including a required free-text textarea, which is the heaviest possible ask for a first contact. There are no required or optional markers anywhere, so a visitor discovers the rules by being rejected, and since the component ships no JavaScript, that rejection is a native browser tooltip. The four service checkboxes are all client-work options, so a recruiter has only "Something else". The one-business-day promise sits roughly 250px above the submit button and is not repeated near it.

**The five highest-impact conversion changes:**

1. Put one number in the hero. The TimeClick traffic figure, attributed.
2. Cut the homepage to three or four selected projects, each ending in a result.
3. Add two or three testimonials, or if you have none, add a line of attributed client context you can defend.
4. Add an email address and a statement of availability.
5. Add a budget or engagement-type field and mark optional fields, so the form filters and reassures instead of interrogating.

"Took timeclick.com from 4,000 to 30,000 monthly organic visitors between December 2025 and April 2026, measured in Google Analytics" is evidence. You own the second sentence. It is on a sub-page. ## 10. Accessibility

Better than most. Verified working: contrast passes throughout both themes; alt text on all 233 images with 23 correctly empty decoratives; one `h1` per page; a working skip link to `#main`; `prefers-reduced-motion` honoured in CSS and in three separate scripts, including one that prevents any video from downloading; the honeypot correctly wrapped in a `visually-hidden` container; keyboard focus visible via a 3px yellow outline at 10.31:1 against the header, and via a 2px yellow border at 11.3:1 on form inputs; the mobile menu sets `aria-expanded`, locks body scroll and closes on Escape.

Six real defects. The first three are contrast failures on focus and skip affordances, which I verified by computing the ratios on the running page.

1. **The skip link is unreadable in dark theme, at 1.10:1.** `Layout.astro:86-87` sets `background: var(--yellow)` with `color: var(--text-header)`. In light theme that is `#111111` on yellow and scores 15.16:1. In dark theme `--text-header` flips to `#f3f1ee`, so it becomes near-white text on yellow. This is the first focusable element on all twenty pages, and it is the one affordance a screen-reader or keyboard user hits first. Fix: change that one value to `var(--on-accent)`, which the codebase already uses for exactly this reason at `global.css:382`.
2. **The focus ring is invisible on the primary button, at 1.27:1.** The global ring is `--yellow-dark #e6cf1c` and the primary button is `--yellow #ffe83d`. Both are literal tokens, so this fails identically in both themes. Tab to "See my work" and the ring effectively disappears. In light theme the same ring is 1.58:1 against the page background, so it is weak everywhere except on dark surfaces, where it scores 11.13:1. Fix: use a dark ring in light contexts, or add a second contrasting outline so the indicator works on any backdrop.
3. **Form fields have no usable focus indicator in light theme.** `Contact.astro:147-151` is the only `outline: none` in the codebase, and it replaces the ring with a yellow border. Measured on the running page, that border scores 1.25:1 against the form panel and 1.09:1 against the field itself in light theme. It works in dark, at 12.42:1. So in light mode a keyboard user cannot tell which of the three fields they are in. Fix: keep the border, and restore an outline behind it.
4. **The mobile menu does not move or trap focus.** I opened it and measured: focus stays on the burger button, and the page behind stays reachable. `Header.astro` handles Escape at line 411 but has no focus move, no focus trap and no `inert` on the background. A keyboard user opening the menu tabs into content they cannot see, on a page that cannot scroll because body overflow is locked. Fix: on open, move focus to the first link; on close, return it to the burger; make the rest of the page `inert` while open.
5. **Heading level skip on two pages.** `/404.html` and `/thanks/` go `h1` straight to `h3`, because the footer uses `h3` for "Social" and "Manny Pachano" as styled column labels. On content pages an `h2` precedes them so it reads correctly. Fix: make the footer labels `h2`, or non-heading elements with a visually-hidden label.
6. **Checkbox touch targets are 25px.** Above the 24px floor, below the 44px comfort target, and they are the controls most likely to be tapped on a phone.

One more worth knowing, though it is cosmetic: `global.css:162` puts `border-radius: 4px` inside the global `:focus-visible` rule, and the contact form's submit button is the one button whose own radius is not class-scoped, so its corners visibly square from 8px to 4px the moment a keyboard user reaches it.

Reduced motion deserves a caveat too. The global rule at `global.css:792-800` zeroes transition and animation durations, which removes the tween but not the displacement, so nine hover transforms still jump to their moved position instantly. `ProjectCard.astro:177-182` is the only component that opts out properly with `transform: none`, and that is the right pattern to copy to the other eight.

## 11. Mobile

No horizontal overflow at 375px, and the layout stacks cleanly. The burger menu is a tidy full-screen overlay. Problems specific to mobile:

- **The page is 20,477px, about 25 screens.** That is a long scroll for a decision that should take two.
- **The hero is 732px of an 812px viewport**, so the first screen is entirely you, and the visitor must scroll a full screen before anything else exists.
- **The lede is six centred lines** at 18px with 1.8 line-height. Left-align and shorten.
- **The "Show 6 more projects" button is the right instinct applied at the wrong layer.** Cards 6 to 11 are hidden below 901px via `display: none`, which usefully stops their videos downloading. But it means the phone visitor sees five projects and the desktop visitor sees eleven, so the curation you already believe in exists only on small screens. Make the curation the same on both, and put the rest behind one link.
- **The mobile menu is five links in a full black screen** with no contact detail and no call to action in a very large empty space.

## 12. Performance

The loading strategy is genuinely good and I want to be clear about that, because the raw numbers look alarming until you see the gating.

**First paint downloads zero bytes of video.** All 11 card videos carry `preload="none"` and no `autoplay` attribute; playback is started by an IntersectionObserver in `Layout.astro:104-124`, which also checks reduced motion, so a visitor with that preference downloads no video at all, ever.

**The cost lands on scroll.** A desktop visitor who scrolls the whole homepage pulls 8.81MB of video in Chrome or Firefox, and 11.97MB in Safari, because seven of the eleven cards ship mp4 only. On a phone the cap is 3.85MB to 5.08MB, since cards 6 to 11 never enter the layout. Video posters, 0.48MB, load unconditionally.

**Real waste, in priority order:**

1. **43 phone screenshots are served at 1170px wide into a slot about 258 CSS px wide.** That is 4.20MB, 27.9% of the entire image budget, and still 2.3x oversized at 2x device pixel ratio. Re-export at 560px.
2. **14.82MB of video and 0.62MB of images are referenced by no page at all**, left from the older carousel approach, and they deploy on every build.
3. **Seven of eleven card videos are mp4-only.** Adding webm would cut the Safari path by roughly a quarter.
4. **The header logo is a 384px file in a 24px slot** with no `height` attribute and no `loading` attribute, on all 20 pages. `Header.astro:18`. Adding `height="35"` removes a small layout shift site-wide.
5. **The invisible hero illustration costs 108KB per homepage visit** for an image almost nobody sees.

Everything else is sound: fonts use `font-display: swap` with the three above-the-fold weights preloaded, aspect ratios reserve space for every media box, total JavaScript is 5,826 bytes across six files, and the theme script is correctly render-blocking to avoid a flash.

## 13. SEO and discoverability

**The site is deliberately unlisted.** Nineteen of nineteen built pages carry `noindex, nofollow, noarchive` in a meta tag, and Netlify sends the same as a header. `robots.txt` deliberately allows crawling so bots can read the directive, which is the technically correct way to do it. This is a considered decision, not an accident.

Everything else in this section is therefore about link previews rather than rankings, and only becomes urgent if you switch indexing on.

- Titles: all 20 unique, none over 60 characters. Good.
- Descriptions: all 20 unique, but 13 fall outside the 120 to 160 character range, 10 of them too long.
- One Open Graph image for all 20 pages, so every case study shares a social card.
- No structured data of any kind. No `Person`, no `WebSite`, no `BreadcrumbList`.
- No sitemap, and `@astrojs/sitemap` is not installed.
- `/404.html` declares a canonical of `/404/`, a URL that does not exist.

**My recommendation.** Decide deliberately. If this is a link-only portfolio, delete the sitemap and structured-data items from your backlog and ignore section 12 of your brief. If you want inbound discovery, removing the directive is a two-line change, and the case study depth you have already written is genuinely competitive content.

## 14. Emotional response

**What the site produces:** mild curiosity, steady trust, and no urgency. The restraint reads as competence. The honesty notes produce real goodwill, because almost nobody labels their own concept work as unsolicited.

**What it does not produce:** conviction. Nothing on the page makes a visitor think "this person will make me money" or "I should talk to them before someone else does". The cause is precise: the site never puts a consequence next to an action. Every summary ends at what you made, not at what changed.

**Where skepticism creeps in.** Three of eleven "Recent projects" are self-initiated, six of six landing pages are concepts, and several concepts are for companies that did not ask. A sympathetic reader sees initiative. A skeptical reader, halfway down, starts wondering how much of this was actually paid. Labelling client work versus self-initiated work would remove that doubt entirely and cost you nothing, because the real client work is strong.

## 15. Competitive differentiation

Against fifty similar portfolios, here is the honest split.

**Generic:** the "Hey, I'm [name]" headline, the skills-and-tools pill grid, the long reverse-chronological wall of projects, the cursor trail, the contact form with name, email and message.

**Distinctive and worth building on:**

1. **A measured SEO outcome.** Developers do not have traffic curves. This is the rarest thing you own.
2. **Unsolicited redesigns, labelled as unsolicited.** Six of them, live, with section-by-section reasoning.
3. **The compare slider** on the landing cards, light against dark, dragged by the visitor.
4. **A footer that makes a falsifiable claim about its own build.** No trackers, under 1MB.
5. **The disclosure discipline** on the invented finance firm.

**The signature to press.** Items 1 and 2 combine into a single recognisable idea: you rebuild a page and then prove the rebuild worked. Nobody else on the shortlist will have both halves.

## What I would not change

The pre-paint theme script, which reads storage safely, follows the operating system only while no choice is stored, and avoids a flash entirely. The literal-versus-semantic token split documented at the top of `global.css`, and in particular the rule that screenshot beds stay light in both themes so a light client site is never framed in dark. That is a sharper idea than most design systems contain.

The colour system and its contrast discipline. The single-typeface restraint. The Walkthrough case study template, which is genuinely better than most agency case studies. The compare slider. The honesty notes on unsolicited and invented work. The reduced-motion handling. The footer's falsifiable build claim. The video loading strategy, which is correct and deliberate. The response-time promise. And the writing itself, which is clear, plain and free of the usual portfolio noise; the problem is what it omits, not how it reads.
